The Deep Mine · build order

What we build, and in what order

Everything settled in the design conversation, sequenced so each piece lands on machinery that already exists and fixes something live before the next one needs it.

Settled premises

  • Rock is a wall, not a material. Mycelium doesn't eat rock, so this is a maze-runner, not a digger.
  • There are no return trips — you are the map. The run ends where it ends; you advance rather than come back.
  • Run 1 is under a minute and already is: 44 water at 2 a dig is 22 digs, measured dry at 52–60 m. Late runs 3–5 minutes, tuned later.
  • Every threat change is mine-only. The campaign keeps its gradual mould and the 116 assertions that guard it.
PHASE 1

Threats become a system

fixes what's broken now

01 Worms drain water instead of eating

They attach and feed on the colony rather than consuming it, so the threat pushes on the clock the run already has instead of needing a death condition of its own.

  • 1 water per 5 seconds per attached worm
  • 5% chance per second of multiplying while feeding — drastically down from today's 0.8 per tick to a cap of 150
  • Attachment is drawn on the strand and counted in the HUD, or it's invisible damage in a new coat
Builds on
stepNematodes, placeNematodes; retires strandsPerBite and breedChance for the mine
Why first
It's the live problem. Four strands a bite with no cooldown against an 800-strand colony is roughly a hundred seconds of watching, and breeding makes it accelerate.

02 Threat sight rings on by default

Show what each worm and cloud can actually sense, so routing around them is a skill rather than a surprise.

Builds on
drawOccludedSight, threatSight — rays already stop at the rock face, so what you see is what it sees
Why here
Small, and it makes the two threats either side of it avoidable instead of arbitrary.

03 Trych infection countdown

The spread stays exactly as it works in the campaign — contact, the cloud is spent and fades, rot creeps along the filaments. What's new is a deadline on top of it.

  • A colony-wide timer starts on first contact
  • Infected strands can't be grown from; clean ones still can
  • Timer ends → the whole colony turns green at once, forced to fruit, run over
  • The ending is a full payout — everything banked is kept, exactly like running dry
  • One timer, per colony, and there is only ever one colony
  • It stops only when every infected strand is gone. Two contact points and you cut one — the clock keeps running. Partial amputation buys nothing.
Builds on
infectNetwork, infectStrandsInMould, amputateAt, mineEndRun
Careful
mine-gated The gradual spread is heavily tuned and the campaign depends on all of it. Add the timer beside it; don't replace it.

04 Consumables

Bought between runs, including Amputate — there is no free answer to the mould. These ship close to 03, because they are what the timer is asking a question about.

Builds on
excrete() and amputateAt() are already fully implemented — they belonged to the card layer and never came across, which is why the mine currently has no counterplay at all
Consequence
Anti-trych items must be preventative, used at range: the cloud is spent the moment it touches you and fades away, so there's nothing left to kill. Anti-worm items are reactive.
Interlock
load-bearing Because Amputate is bought, an early player who meets mould with nothing in the bag simply loses the run — which is fine only because the ending is a full payout. Those two decisions hold each other up; changing either one alone makes early mould brutal.
PHASE 2

The run gets a shape

depth means something

05 Heat tolerance, as depth gating

It gets hotter as you descend, and heat is priced rather than enforced: below your tolerance limit, every strand costs more water, and more again the further past the limit you go.

  • Nothing burns off. A dig below tolerance succeeds — it just costs more, so the player is never punished after the fact for a move the game let them make
  • The wall is economic: you can always go one layer deeper than you can afford to, and find out what that costs
  • It lands on the fuel clock, which is the pressure the run already has — no second system
Builds on
coreY, growFloorY and the earth ramp to red — the molten floor and its art already exist, and mineGrowCost is a single function. This turns a picture into a rule.
Why here
The cheapest real gate available, and it gives the shop something to sell that isn't just more of the same.

06 More material types

Named materials with band affinities, so an upgrade needs a particular thing and the maze is worth reading.

Builds on
foodKind already carries three art kinds; orePerPile already varies by band
Depends on
pairs with 08 Without a compass, "find garnet" is a random walk. These two hold each other up — don't ship this one alone.
PHASE 3

The journey east

the game gets a point

07 Green islands and the promised land

Islands scattered eastward. Reach one, resurface and spore, and the next run starts from there. Surface resources barely fund the next leg, so the first runs have to go down to afford going right.

Why it matters
It gives the world a second axis — depth is currently the only one — and it turns "no return trip" from a missing feature into the structure of the game.
Shape
A fresh world per leg, seeded by journey and leg index, hill at the west and island at the east. Keeps the 504-column map that already works and makes "how far east am I" a small integer rather than a coordinate.

08 Compasses, bought per resource

A needle for the next island, and separately purchased tech for each material you want to track. Both are bought — nothing here is given. Selling information rather than power is the most distinctive progression idea in the game.

A player with no compass is not lost, because east is the standing instruction: the promised land is that way and always was. What the compass sells is precision — exact bearing and distance — which is a much easier thing to price than the difference between having a direction and having none.

  • Rungs fall out of one feature: direction → direction and distance → points at the biggest deposit rather than the nearest
  • It must point through rock. A bearing keeps the maze — "I know it's that way, now find the corridor". A route would make the maze stop mattering.
Why last
It's what makes 06 and 07 legible, so it wants both of them to exist first.
PHASE 4

Economy and progression

dial it in

09 Price everything against the journey

One pass over costs, yields and upgrade ladders once the shape is real.

  • Run 1 stays around 45 seconds and banks one upgrade — band-1 ore pays 3 P a seam against a 10 P cheapest buy, so this is already close
  • Late runs land at 3–5 minutes
  • The key ratio is shallow-to-deep material value: get it wrong either way and one of the two axes dies

Decisions taken

Settled, with the reasoning, so none of it gets re-litigated.

Heat is priced, not enforced

Below your tolerance limit every strand costs more water, and more again the deeper you go. Nothing burns off. A dig the game allows always succeeds — you simply find out what it cost, rather than being punished after the fact for a move it let you make.

Amputate is bought

There is no free answer to the mould. This is only fair because the forced fruiting pays out in full — meeting mould with an empty bag ends the run early rather than taking anything away. The two decisions are a pair.

A fresh world per leg

Seeded by journey and leg index, hill at the west, island at the east. The 504-column streamed map stays exactly as it is, and eastward progress is a small integer instead of a coordinate in an ever-wider world.

The island compass is bought too

Nothing is given. A player without one still knows to go east — that is the goal and always was — so what is being sold is precision, not the difference between having a direction and having none.

One infection timer, per colony

There is only ever one colony, so one deadline. It stops only when every infected strand is gone: cut one of two contact points and the clock keeps running. Partial amputation buys nothing, which is what makes "cut all of it" a real decision.

Still to settle

Tuning rather than design — none of it blocks starting.

The heat cost curve

Linear per metre past the limit, or accelerating? Accelerating gives a sharper felt edge to each tolerance upgrade; linear is easier to price against the ore ladder.

How a leg escalates

Each leg east presumably runs deeper bands and heavier threats. Whether that is a curve or a hand-authored table is the same question the campaign answered with LEVEL_THREATS, and the answer there was a table for the first eleven.

How long a late run is

Three to five minutes, roughly. Worth leaving open until the costs pass, since it is the output of the economy rather than an input to it.

Numbers quoted are measured from the current build, not estimated.
